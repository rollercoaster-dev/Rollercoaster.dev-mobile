# Product & app naming — how to get to a name that fits

Researched 2026-10-16 against USPTO and EUIPO trademark guidance, Apple App Store Review
Guidelines + product-page docs, Google Play policy/Console help, the published methods of
Igor, Lexicon (David Placek), Catchword, A Hundred Monkeys, Eat My Words (SMILE & SCRATCH),
First Round Review (Arielle Jackson), Every/Onym (Willem Van Lancker), peer-reviewed
bouba/kiki papers, and first-party naming accounts from Slack, Focusmate, Llama Life, Finch.
Secondary sources are flagged inline. Nothing here names the app; it gives the process.

## TL;DR

- Write a one-paragraph positioning + a naming brief first. Every agency starts here; skipping it is the #1 failure mode ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [Igor](https://igorinternational.com/process/naming-agency-naming-agencies.php), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- Pick the name _type_ deliberately: suggestive/evocative real words are the sweet spot between "trademarkable" and "needs no explanation" ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks), [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- Generate by _themes/territories_, not words. Aim for hundreds to low thousands of candidates; shortlist ~10 % ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [Placek](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- Separate generation from judgment. Ban evaluation during the generative phase ([Placek](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
- Score a shortlist of 10–25 against a fixed rubric (below); trademark "not likely available" is a hard 0 ([Igor](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/blank-name-evaluation-chart.php)).
- Screen: TM knockout (USPTO/EUIPO/DPMA/TMview) → app-store collision → domain/handles → DE/EN/ES/FR meaning check. Do this on 3–5 finalists, not one ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks)).
- Validate with the recall test (say it, ask people next day) and the competitor test ("a rival just launched as X, reaction?"). Never ask "which do you like?" ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/), [Placek](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek), [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- Keep the store title ≤ 30 chars on both stores; put the descriptive keywords in the subtitle/short description, not the name ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/), [Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/113469)).

## 1. Name types and trademark strength

Takeaway: the more a name _describes_, the weaker it is legally and the more it blends in; the more it _invents_, the more marketing it needs. Suggestive is the middle.

Trademark strength spectrum, as the USPTO publishes it ([Strong trademarks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks)):

- **Fanciful** — invented words, meaning only for the product. Exxon, Pepsi. Strongest.
- **Arbitrary** — real words unrelated to the product. Apple for computers.
- **Suggestive** — hints at a quality without stating it. Coppertone for sun-tan products.
- **Descriptive** — "merely describe some aspect"; registrable only after acquired distinctiveness. "Creamy" for yogurt. Weak.
- **Generic** — the common name of the thing; never a trademark. "Bicycle" for bicycles.
- USPTO's own choosing advice: pick inherently distinctive; ask whether the public will "remember, pronounce, and spell it"; check the translation if marketing abroad ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks)).

EU equivalent (Regulation 2017/1001 Art. 7, as applied in the EUIPO Guidelines Part B §4, v1.4 of 31/03/2024 — the copy fetched is watermarked "Obsolete", so a newer revision exists; wording below is stable case-law) ([EUIPO Guidelines](https://guidelines.euipo.europa.eu/)):

- Distinctive = "serves to identify the goods and/or services ... as originating from a particular undertaking". A _minimum_ degree suffices.
- Descriptive (Art. 7(1)(c)) = meaning "immediately perceived by the relevant public as providing information about the goods and services"; the link must be "sufficiently direct and specific ... understood without further reflection". Descriptive ⇒ also non-distinctive.
- "Only suggestive or allusive" terms are _not_ caught by 7(1)(c). That is the EU's version of the suggestive sweet spot.
- Laudatory words (ECO, "best", "simple") get refused as non-distinctive ([EUIPO draft chapter, 2017](https://euipo.europa.eu/tunnel-web/secure/webdav/guest/document_library/contentPdfs/trade_marks/Draft_Guidelines_WP_1_2017/07_part_b_examination_section_4_AG_chap_3_article_71-b_tc_2017_en.pdf)).

Agency taxonomies layered on top of the legal one:

- Igor: **functional/descriptive**, **invented** (Greek/Latin roots _or_ "poetic constructions based on the rhythm and experience of saying them"), **experiential** (connect to a real everyday experience), **evocative** (evoke positioning rather than the goods). Step one is choosing which of the four the name must be ([Igor](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/name-development-product-company.php)).
- Catchword: descriptive / suggestive / "empty vessel" (arbitrary + fanciful) / foreign-language / acronyms. Says suggestive is "the most popular kind of brand name"; advises against acronyms. By _construction_: real words, coined, truncated (Cisco, FedEx), compounds (Facebook, DreamWorks), alphanumerics ([Catchword guide](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- First Round adds the full construction list: phrases, affixes, blends, fragments, misspellings, foreign words, onomatopoeia, eponyms, people/places ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- Lexicon: three things carry a name's meaning — **semantics, structure, sound symbolism**. Invented names get all their meaning from the last two (Dasani) ([Lexicon](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/)).

## 2. Frameworks, as published by their owners

Takeaway: all agree on brief → volume → shortlist → screen → decide; they differ on how to judge.

**Igor — "Building the Perfect Beast" naming guide** ([download page](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/brand-name-naming-agency-startup.php); process and chart pages are public):

- Process: Positioning → Competitive analysis (map the tone/strength of rival names) → Name development → Trademark prescreen on every name before presenting ([Igor process](https://igorinternational.com/process/naming-agency-naming-agencies.php)).
- Evaluation chart: 9 criteria × 10 pts = 90 max. Appearance, Distinctive, Depth (layers of meaning that unfold over time), Energy, Humanity ("imagine it as a child's nickname"), Positioning, Sound, "33" (word-of-mouth magic), Trademark (fixed: 10 likely available / 5 maybe / 0 not — and 0 removes the name) ([Igor chart](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/blank-name-evaluation-chart.php)).

**Lexicon Branding / David Placek** (BlackBerry, Swiffer, Pentium, Sonos, Azure, Windsurf):

- Five-step process: avoid groupthink (small teams, "traditional brainstorming isn't effective") → make it memorable → ensure trademarkable (beyond knockout searches) → check linguistics globally → use research as a _decision tool_, not a popularity contest (Pentium "was not the most popular candidate") ([Lexicon process](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/)).
- Criteria: "remarkably distinctive"; the **competitor wince test** — cut it if rivals would shrug; relevance via attitude/association, not description; "processing power" (high-frequency word parts); domains matter less ([Lexicon process](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/)).
- Sound symbolism claims (Lexicon's proprietary research, not independently verified): b = reliable, v = vibrant, z = attention, x = innovation; BlackBerry's full consonants b/l/r = feature-packed, thin vowels = light device ([Lexicon](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/), [Lenny's podcast summary](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
- Working method: three teams, one with the real brief, one briefed as a competitor, one on an unrelated category (Windsurf came from a team listing "flow" concepts). Expect 1,000–1,500 names to find gems. Polarization is the signal — Andy Grove on Pentium: "that tells me there's energy". Compounds "1 + 1 = 3". "Get the right name first, then solve for the domain" ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).

**Eat My Words / Alexandra Watkins — SMILE & SCRATCH** ([Eat My Words](https://eatmywords.com/smile-scratch-test/); from her book _Hello, My Name Is Awesome_):

- SMILE (must have): **S**uggestive, **M**emorable (associated with the familiar), **I**magery, **L**egs (lends itself to a theme), **E**motional.
- SCRATCH (deal-breakers): **S**pelling-challenged, **C**opycat, **R**estrictive, **A**nnoying (forced), **T**ame (flat/descriptive), **C**urse of knowledge (insiders only), **H**ard to pronounce.

**Marty Neumeier — 7 criteria** (from _ZAG_/_The Brand Gap_; only secondary reproductions found, see §9): distinctive ("speech-stream visibility" — the ear picks it out of a sentence), brief (≤ 4 syllables or people abbreviate it), appropriate but not generic, easy to spell, satisfying to pronounce, suitable for "brandplay"/extendable, legally defensible ([Mark Ramsey Media repost](https://www.markramseymedia.com/amp/what-makes-a-good-radio-station-name), [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/whats-in-a-name/)).

**Catchword — "Creating the Perfect Name" guide** ([PDF](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)):

- Brief questions: what are you naming; how will it be used; which arena; competitor names and patterns; audience; the one difference they care about; messages; tonality/construction; open to other languages; target countries; legal/linguistic constraints; family/naming conventions; domain needed?
- Creative: build a _project vocabulary_ (synonyms per message bucket, translations, affixes, truncations) → "blue-sky territory" (metaphors, unexpected pairings, founder hobbies, song titles) → ~2,000 names per project.
- Selection: shortlist ~10 %, re-prune with fresh eyes days later. 10 selection guidelines: don't rush (4–6 weeks + legal); play the field (screen several); don't decide by committee; don't expect the name to tell the whole story; don't get hung up on the exact .com; trust the audience's intelligence; forget "virgin" names (coexistence in unrelated classes is normal); get past personal associations; embrace the unusual ("Would CoffeeWorld have gotten the same traction as Starbucks?"); avoid perfectionism.
- 10 criteria: magnetism, distinctiveness, brand fit, accessibility (say + spell), longevity, conciseness, euphony, appropriateness (no serious negative meaning in a major language), consistency, protectability.
- Research rule: "Don't ask people which name they like best." Consumers pick the most literal name ("Coffee Connection" over "Starbucks").

**A Hundred Monkeys** ([site](https://www.ahundredmonkeys.com/naming/)):

- "About 20 % of our job is generating names. The other 80 % is getting a group of people to make a subjective decision together."
- Test: does it get "a smile or a raised eyebrow"? Form: "a word or two, a short phrase at most." Longer names stand out and clear trademark/URL more easily. Every name ships with a story.
- Screening order: trademark → URL → cultural.

**First Round Review / Arielle Jackson** — the most complete DIY playbook ([How to pick a company name](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)):

- Expect ≥ 1 month. Incorporate under a placeholder "so outlandish that it could never see the light of day."
- Positioning statement → 12-question brief → "namestorm" around 8–10 themes (animal groups, mythology, "just say it") → everything in a spreadsheet → shortlist 10–25, stoplight-rated → each founder independently picks top 3 → test → clear.
- Good signs: polarizing, grows on you, works on two levels. Bad: many similar names exist, boring, easy to mock, too narrow for later products.
- "If you ask a bunch of people to help pick a name, you're likely to end up with a least common denominator name."

**Willem Van Lancker (Onym / Terrain)** ([Every](https://every.to/thesis/a-founder-s-guide-to-finding-a-great-name), [Onym guide](https://guide.onym.co/)):

- Steps: orient → explore → distill → validate → commit → execute.
- Judge on depth, temperature (warm vs cold), voice (aloud), visual (small screens, capitalization), differentiation, and "special wrongness" — "an unforgettable newness. A new shape. 1+1=3."
- Onym is an open-source directory of naming tools (dictionaries, generators, TM/domain/handle checkers, etymologies, cautionary tales).

## 3. The practical process, step by step

Takeaway: six stages; most people skip 1 and 5 and over-invest in 3.

1. **Brief** (1–2 pages). Positioning: for whom, which need, which category, key benefit, difference. Add the Catchword questions above. Decide the Igor _type_ (functional / invented / experiential / evocative) and what the name must _do_ ([Igor](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/name-development-product-company.php), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)). Map competitor names to find the empty tonal space ([Igor](https://igorinternational.com/process/naming-agency-naming-agencies.php), [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
2. **Territories.** 8–10 themes before any single name (metaphor families, places, mythology, materials, verbs, "plain statement") ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/); Catchword's "blue-sky territory" and mood boards).
3. **Divergent generation.** Volume: Catchword ~2,000; Placek 1,000–1,500 ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)). No judging while generating. Techniques with a source:
   - Project vocabulary → synonyms, antonyms, translations (Latin, Italian, Hawaiian...), prefixes/suffixes, truncations ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
   - Compounds/blends for "1 + 1 = 3" associations (PowerBook, Facebook) ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
   - Structure cues: familiar endings (-ium), rhythm, repeated sounds (Sonos, KitKat) ([Lexicon](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/), [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
   - Sound symbolism, the only part with peer-reviewed backing: round/soft sounds pair with round shapes, sharp sounds with spiky ones (Ramachandran & Hubbard 2001, _J. Consciousness Studies_ 8(12):3–34 — citation only, paper not fetched). Replicated online across 25 languages, 9 families, 10 writing systems ([Ćwiek et al. 2022, _Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B_](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2959625)); the effect tracks the sound's frequency, not mouth shape ([Passi & Arun 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615921/)). Practical use: a calm/soft product wants b/l/m/o/u; a sharp/fast one k/t/i.
   - Separate teams/sessions with different briefs to escape groupthink ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
   - AI: use for narrow prompts ("lesser-known sailing terms for X"), never "give me company names" ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
4. **Shortlist.** Keep ~10 %, sleep on it, prune again ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)). Then 10–25 scored with the rubric in §5; founders pick top 3 independently ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
5. **Screening** (on 3–5 finalists together, never one — [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)):
   - TM knockout: USPTO Trademark Search + TMOG, EUIPO/TMview, DPMA, Global Brand Database; then common-law web search. Similarity counts in "sound, appearance, or meaning"; marks "don't have to be identical to be confusingly similar" ([USPTO clearance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks), [USPTO likelihood of confusion](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion)). Relevant Nice classes for an app: 9 (software) and 42 (SaaS) — the 45-class system per [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf).
   - App-store collision: search both stores for the name and near-spellings; Apple forbids using "another developer's icon, brand, or product name" and names "confusingly similar" to Apple apps ([App Review 4.1(c), 5.2.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
   - Domain + handles: get the name right, then solve the domain with a descriptor/prefix/other TLD ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/), [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
   - Linguistic check DE/EN/ES/FR: ask > 1 native speaker per language, in the target audience, and probe vague answers. Catchword's kills: COZM ≈ "kotzen" (DE, vomit), REEGO ≈ "rego" (MX-ES), KIZMO ≈ "kizumono" (JP) ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
   - Spell-on-hearing: say it once by voice message; ask them to type it. Apple's own advice is "easy to spell" ([Apple product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)).
6. **Validation + legal clearance.** Recall test (talk about 3 finalists, ask next day which they remember) and "what does this name communicate?" with current and uninitiated users ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)). Competitor test instead of "do you like it?" ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)). Present all names identically, no logos ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)). Then an attorney review — every source says the DIY search is not clearance ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks), [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).

## 4. App-store constraints

Takeaway: 30 characters on both stores, no keyword-stuffing in the name, descriptive copy goes in subtitle/short description.

- **Apple**: "App names must be limited to 30 characters"; don't pack metadata with "trademarked terms, popular app names, pricing information, or other irrelevant phrases" (Guideline 2.3.7). Subtitles must not reference other apps or make unverifiable claims. No other developer's product name (4.1(c)); nothing confusingly similar to Apple's own apps (5.2.5); no "for kids" terms outside the Kids category (2.3.8) ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- Apple's product-page advice: "Choose a simple, memorable name that is easy to spell and hints at what your app does. Be distinctive. Avoid names that use generic terms or are too similar to existing app names." Subtitle (also ≤ 30) is where you "explain the value of your app in greater detail" ([Apple product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)).
- **Google Play**: title "must be 30 characters or less"; no emoji/repeated symbols; "Avoid ALL CAPS unless it is part of your brand name"; no ranking/price/promo words ("#1", "Best", "free", "New") ([Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842)). Console limits: name 30, short description 80, full description 4,000 ([Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/113469)).
- Pattern in the wild: ND apps use the 30 chars as `Brand: keyword keyword`, e.g. the App Store listing "ADHD Organizer: Llama Life" (26 chars) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750)). The brand stays short; the descriptor rides alongside.

## 5. How others did it (first-party accounts only)

Takeaway: real-word, suggestive names chosen for feel, then rationalised; domain/TM availability decided several of them.

- **Slack** — "Slack" was the codename on Stewart Butterfield's pitch deck ("codename /slack"). Reactions: "You're calling it Slack?" The team searched for something memorable _and_ available: Cedar and Pecan (cute, meaningless on reflection), Honeycomb (trademark-blocked), Circuit, Superset (taken), Loop.in, Kitchn. Internal use ("Slack that to me") made it feel natural. slack.com bought June 2013 after offers of $8K → $25K → $60K. The "Searchable Log of All Conversation and Knowledge" backronym is contested inside Slack itself ([Building Slack, by Slack staff](https://buildingslack.com/we-cant-call-it-slack/)); Wikipedia records Butterfield giving the acronym in 2012, replacing codename "linefeed" ([Wikipedia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slack_(software)>), citing Business Insider 2016). Lesson: a loose, real word with a built-in double meaning, plus a backronym for the sceptics.
- **Focusmate** — founder Taylor Jacobson: not the first name considered; "it was the first one where I could buy the domain name for a reasonable price and get the trademark" ([Smart Branding interview](https://smartbranding.com/?p=1432)). Compound of benefit + relationship noun.
- **Llama Life** (ADHD) — from founder Marie Ng's 2018 trip to a Peruvian village with no electricity but many llamas; she wanted the name to carry how "peaceful, calm and content" the locals were ([Llama Life help centre](https://intercom.help/llama-life/en/articles/6455725-why-the-name)). Arbitrary animal + aspirational noun; alliterative.
- **Finch** (self-care, popular with ND users) — co-founder Nino was reading _To Kill a Mockingbird_; Atticus Finch → finches, which "work better with others; they can't live on their own"; "Pino" was an earlier candidate (secondary: a Substack paraphrasing a co-founder interview and the Finch community AMA, [source](https://dishabhatia.substack.com/p/the-self-care-bird-app-finch)). The name became the mascot and the whole product metaphor.
- **Amie** (calendar) — pronounced like "Amy", not French _ami_; the company has to say so in interviews ([Inverse](https://inverse.com/tech/amie-calendar-app-dennis-muller-ceo-interview)). Lesson: a name that needs a pronunciation note costs you forever.
- **Basecamp / 37signals** — company named after 37 candidate extraterrestrial radio signals; renamed to its product Basecamp in 2014; renamed back in 2022 because "calling our company Basecamp just doesn't make a lot of sense when we make more than just Basecamp" ([Wikipedia summary of Fried's announcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37signals)). Lesson: product name ≠ company name; keep the product name specific.
- **Lexicon's cases** — BlackBerry: founders "thought the name was 'crazy'"; Azure: Microsoft called it "a dumb name"; Sonos rejected at first for "not being 'entertainment' enough"; Windsurf came from a team told nothing about IDEs ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)). Lesson: initial discomfort is normal for the names that win.
- **ND / ADHD app cohort** (Tiimo, Finch, Goblin.tools, Llama Life, Sunsama, Routinery, Focusmate, Inflow) — only Llama Life, Finch and Focusmate publish an origin (above). Observed pattern (my analysis, not sourced): none is descriptive; most are _warm, soft-sounding real or near-real words_ (lots of l/m/n/o), often a creature or companion (Finch, Llama, Goblin, -mate) or a blend with a familiar suffix (Routine+ry, Sun+sama, In+flow, Tiimo ≈ time/team). They signal calm and companionship rather than productivity. Descriptive keywords then live in the store subtitle (§4).

## 6. Evaluation rubric (score 0–2 each; TM is a gate)

Takeaway: score every finalist the same way, then look at the spread, not the sum.

- **Distinctive in its category** — stands out from competitor names _and_ from ordinary words in a sentence. Igor "Distinctive"; Neumeier "speech-stream visibility"; Catchword "Distinctiveness"; Lexicon "competitor wince" ([Igor](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/blank-name-evaluation-chart.php), [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [Lexicon](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/)).
- **Suggestive, not descriptive** — hints at benefit/feel; survives Art. 7(1)(c)/USPTO "descriptive". Watkins "S"; Neumeier "appropriate but not generic"; Igor "Positioning" ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks), [Eat My Words](https://eatmywords.com/smile-scratch-test/)).
- **Depth / legs** — more than one layer; carries a theme, a mascot, an icon. Igor "Depth"; Watkins "L" + "I"; Van Lancker "depth" ([Igor](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/blank-name-evaluation-chart.php), [Every](https://every.to/thesis/a-founder-s-guide-to-finding-a-great-name)).
- **Emotional / warm** — "a smile or a raised eyebrow"; works as a child's nickname. Igor "Humanity"; Watkins "E"; A Hundred Monkeys; Van Lancker "temperature" ([A Hundred Monkeys](https://www.ahundredmonkeys.com/naming/)).
- **Short** — ≤ 4 syllables (Neumeier), "a word or two" (A Hundred Monkeys), fits 30 chars with room for a descriptor (Apple/Google).
- **Pronounceable on sight, one way** — Watkins "H"; Catchword "Accessibility"; Neumeier "satisfying to pronounce"; the Amie problem.
- **Spellable on hearing** — Watkins "S(pelling-challenged)"; Apple "easy to spell"; test it by voice message.
- **Sounds right** — euphony, sound symbolism matches the product's feel. Igor "Sound"; Catchword "Euphony"; Lexicon ([Lexicon](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/)).
- **Looks right** — in an icon, a 2-line wordmark, lowercase in a sentence. Igor "Appearance"; Van Lancker "visual".
- **Works in a sentence** — "open X", "I'll X it", "my X". Neumeier "brandplay"; Slack's "Slack that to me" test ([Building Slack](https://buildingslack.com/we-cant-call-it-slack/)).
- **No bad meaning in DE/EN/ES/FR** (and no slang hit) — Catchword "Appropriateness"; USPTO translation advice; Lexicon step 4.
- **Not restrictive** — still fits if the app grows past goals/tasks. Watkins "R"; Catchword "Longevity"; First Round "too narrow".
- **Not a copycat / not trend-shaped** — no -ly/-ify/dropped-vowel look-alikes. Watkins "C"; Catchword "Longevity: will it sound dated".
- **Energy / "33"** — would you tell someone about it unprompted? Igor "Energy" + "33"; Van Lancker "special wrongness".
- **GATE — Trademark** — knockout in USPTO + TMview/EUIPO + DPMA, Nice classes 9/42. Likely available = pass; maybe = flag for attorney; not likely = out ([Igor](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/blank-name-evaluation-chart.php), [USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks)).
- **GATE — App-store collision** — no identical or confusingly similar app title in the Productivity/Health categories on either store ([App Review 4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- **Weighted** — Domain (some sensible form) and handles. Deliberately _not_ a gate ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).

Reading the scores: a flat 1-everywhere name is "tame"; a 2/0 spread with a polarised room is often the winner ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).

## 7. Anti-patterns the sources call out

Takeaway: most bad names come from choosing by committee or by domain availability.

- **Purely descriptive** — unprotectable and invisible. "Advanced Optics", "Coffee Connection" ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks)).
- **Spelling-challenged / misspelled real words** — "looks like a typo" ([Eat My Words](https://eatmywords.com/smile-scratch-test/)); Catchword's "Jwaala, Xoopit, Myngle, Fairtilizer — don't go there."
- **Needs explanation / insider-only** — "Curse of knowledge" ([Eat My Words](https://eatmywords.com/smile-scratch-test/)). Yet don't expect the name to tell the whole story either — context (subtitle, icon) does that ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- **Copycat / blends-in** — "There's Alaska, American, United, Delta… and then there's JetBlue" ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)); many similar names existing is a bad sign ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- **Trend-dated** — "Will it sound dated if naming trends change? (RIP, Napster.)" ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)). The -ly/-ify/-r wave is the current version of this.
- **Acronyms/initialisms** — "often forced or confusing" ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- **Restrictive** — "Book World" vs "Amazon" ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)); limits future growth ([Eat My Words](https://eatmywords.com/smile-scratch-test/)).
- **Decided by committee / popular vote** — produces the lowest-common-denominator name ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- **Letting the exact .com decide** — "Don't let the tail wag the dog" ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)); domains matter less in a search-driven world ([Lexicon](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/)).
- **Rejecting for personal or "virgin" reasons** — your cousin's name, or "someone used it in another industry" ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)); same name can coexist across unrelated classes ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- **Falling for one name before screening** — pick several and screen them together; use an outlandish placeholder meanwhile ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- **Keyword-stuffed store titles** — grounds for rejection on both stores ([Apple 2.3.7](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842)).

## 8. Tools (official sites verified reachable on 2026-10-16)

Takeaway: free official registers cover the knockout search; pay a lawyer only for the finalist.

- **Trademark** — USPTO Trademark Search (replaced TESS Nov 2023): [tmsearch.uspto.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) via [USPTO search hub](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search). EUIPO eSearch plus: [euipo.europa.eu/eSearch](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/). TMview (EU national offices + EUIPO + partners): [tmdn.org/tmview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/). DPMAregister (DE, also shows EU + international marks; Basis/Erweitert/Experte modes): [register.dpma.de](https://register.dpma.de/DPMAregister/marke/einsteiger). WIPO Global Brand Database: [branddb.wipo.int](https://branddb.wipo.int/). The USPTO itself lists Madrid Monitor, Global Brand Database, EUIPO and TMview as the international legs of a clearance search ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks)).
- **Domain** — [Instant Domain Search](https://instantdomainsearch.com/) (live availability across TLDs). Catchword's domain-buying path: Sedo/Afternic/GoDaddy, escrow via Escrow.com ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- **Handles** — [namechk.com](https://namechk.com/) (site up, returns 403 to bots; content not verified). Onym lists KnowEm and Namestation as alternatives ([Onym](https://guide.onym.co/)).
- **App-store search** — search both stores by hand for the name, near-spellings and homophones; no official API for title collision.
- **Linguistic / pronunciation** — Forvo (native-speaker pronunciations), Urban Dictionary (slang), Google Translate / Linguee for DE/ES/FR back-translation — all per Onym's vetting list ([Onym](https://guide.onym.co/)). Word Safety (wordsafety.com) was unreachable on the day; not verified.
- **Generation** — Onym's brainstorming set: OneLook (reverse dictionary + thesaurus), Wordnik, RhymeZone, Google Ngram Viewer, Wordoid, Panabee ([Onym](https://guide.onym.co/)). Catchword: thesaurus + translation + affix play ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- **Reading** — Igor's free 90-page guide (download gate on [igorinternational.com](https://www.igorinternational.com/process/brand-name-naming-agency-startup.php)); Catchword's guide PDF (linked above); Watkins, _Hello, My Name Is Awesome_; Neumeier, _ZAG_ / _The Brand Gap_.

## 9. Solo-founder plan (ADHD-sized sessions, ~2 weeks)

Takeaway: eight short sessions, one output each, nothing else in that session.

- **S1 (45 min) Brief.** One-paragraph positioning + answers to the Catchword/First Round brief questions. Choose the Igor type. Write a deliberately silly placeholder name and use it until S8 ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/), [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf)).
- **S2 (30 min) Competitor map.** List 20 rival app names; tag each descriptive/suggestive/invented and warm/cold. Mark the empty quadrant ([Igor](https://igorinternational.com/process/naming-agency-naming-agencies.php)).
- **S3 (30 min) Territories.** 8–10 themes; two lines each on why they fit the positioning ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/)).
- **S4–S5 (2 × 45 min) Generate.** One spreadsheet column per territory; 300+ rows total; no deleting, no rating. Use thesaurus, translations, affixes, compounds, sound-first nonsense ([Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CW_NamingGuide_100914.pdf), [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
- **S6 (30 min, ≥ 2 days later) Shortlist.** Keep ~10 %, then score 10–25 with the §6 rubric. Cut to 5.
- **S7 (60 min) Screen.** For each of the 5: tmsearch.uspto.gov + TMview + DPMAregister (classes 9/42), both app stores, Instant Domain Search, handles, DE/EN/ES/FR back-translation. Record pass/flag/fail ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks)).
- **S8 (async) Validate.** Voice-message the 3 survivors to 5 people; ask them to type each back; next day ask which they remember and what it suggests. Run the competitor test on 2 people ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-pick-company-name/), [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/naming-expert-david-placek)).
- **Decide.** Pick the polarising one that passed gates, not the one nobody objected to. Then attorney knockout opinion, then register the domain/handles and file the store listing with `Brand: descriptor` ≤ 30 chars.
